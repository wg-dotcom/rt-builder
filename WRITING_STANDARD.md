# Jesus Pacheco: writing standard

Updated October 7, 2026. Applies to every page, guide, candidate introduction,
note, summary, email, and generated text written for Jesus. Read this before
writing and again before publishing. This supersedes older voice examples.

## The voice

Sound like Jesus explaining something to someone he knows. Direct, warm,
observant, and confident. A little elegance. No corporate performance.
Use natural contractions and varied sentence lengths. Clean up the typing,
not the personality. Do not add deliberate errors, rambling, or repetition.

Start with the useful point. Give the specific reason. Stop when the reader
has what they need. The point is to help someone decide, not to fill a page.

## What makes a sentence worth keeping

- It adds a relevant fact, a clear judgment, an honest limit, or a next step.
- The judgment follows from evidence the reader can understand.
- It names the actor and the action. Numbers have a source and a meaning.
- It says something specific to this person, role, member, or decision.

If a sentence could sit under any candidate's name, rewrite it or cut it.
Do not restate the same fact in the intro, fit note, highlights, and conclusion.
The résumé can retain the detailed record. Our commentary must add judgment.

## Cut these patterns

- Canned praise: proven track record, ideal candidate, brings a wealth of,
  seasoned professional, robust skill set, stands out, exciting opportunity.
- Sales theater: game changer, powerhouse, unicorn, Swiss Army knife,
  the adult in the room, quiet competence, no hand-holding, hit the ground running.
- Forced punchlines: it's Tuesday, not résumé keywords, the same muscle,
  that's not X, that's Y, hand them a mess and watch what happens.
- Unsupported promises: no ramp-up, ready from day one, perfect fit,
  will transform the business, guaranteed to deliver.
- Empty coaching: be confident, show your passion, own the room, just be yourself.
- Padding: needless transitions, repeated headings, recap paragraphs that add
  nothing, and a clever closing line attached to every paragraph.
- Decorative em dashes and arrows in prose. Use ordinary sentences instead.

These are editing signals, not an AI detector. Plain language is the goal.
Do not claim that writing is human-authored or that it can pass a detector.

## Candidate pages

One introduction per candidate. Maximum 70 words; less is usually enough.
Use two or three relevant facts and explain their connection to the job.
Differentiate candidates through the evidence, not invented personas or
forced variation in opening words. Do not repeat salary, location, and years
just because they already appear elsewhere on the card.

Keep a material gap if it changes the decision. Phrase it calmly and state
what needs confirmation. Never hide uncertainty to make a profile sell better.
Never imply that Jesus interviewed, verified, watched, or met someone unless
the record confirms it. Never invent experience, outcomes, availability,
English fluency, motivation, software mastery, or minimal training needs.

Example using supplied facts only:
"Andrea coordinated 200 daily ride requests and wrote the dispatch team's
SOPs. That experience is relevant to the scheduling and process work here.
I'd ask how much of the workflow redesign Andrea owned personally."

Do not rewrite raw CVs, source quotes, job titles, dates, tools, legal terms,
or financial definitions to make them sound like Jesus. Edit our commentary.
Keep approved biographies unchanged unless Jesus asks to change them.
Never infer gender. Preserve the established anonymization and privacy rules.

## Guides, reports, and internal pages

Give people a clear plan: what matters, who owns it, and what happens next.
For interview prep, use a real example from the candidate's background and
the question it can answer. Do not manufacture first-person interview answers.
For leadership pages, separate facts, interpretation, and open decisions.
Do not turn an estimate, invoice, offer, or pipeline number into collected cash.

Use "member" for Sagan's accounts. Preserve "client" when it is part of a
source quote, job title, or a candidate's work serving another business's clients.
Humor is optional. Use it only when it fits naturally. Never force a mate or
mountain joke into unrelated copy. Keep Jesus's approved advisor biography.

## Before publishing

Read all changed commentary out loud. Could Jesus say it without sounding like
he is reading a script? Is each claim supported? Is any useful uncertainty lost?
Does each section add something? Are the next step and responsible person clear?
Cut anything that fails. A banned-word scan helps, but it does not replace review.

Keep this rule in the builder prompts and publish checks, not only in chat.
Apply it to revisions and new pages. No future page is exempt by format.
